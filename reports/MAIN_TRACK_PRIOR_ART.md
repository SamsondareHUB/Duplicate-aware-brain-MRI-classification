## B. Answer to the central question
**Has the main scientific question already been answered?** The sub-question *"does image-level/slice-level splitting inflate accuracy relative to patient-/subject-disjoint evaluation?"* has been answered repeatedly across OCT, brain MRI, pathology, connectomics and CT (#2–#6), and the specific Figshare brain-tumor dataset has been audited for patient and duplicate contamination two weeks before this review (#1). A paper whose headline is "patient overlap inflates accuracy/calibration on brain MRI" is **not novel**.

**Is there an open gap?** In what we could find, nothing quantifies *whether evaluation dependence changes the reliability of model-selection conclusions* — i.e. the sampling distribution of the model ranking under dependent vs independent evaluation, with split- and training-seed variability. #1 shows architecture ordering changes across evaluation conditions (3 seeds, no patient-disjoint arm, no cluster inference); #10 and #13 treat ranking stability in general. That gap is real but **narrow, and our current evidence does not fill it** (one split seed, one training seed, top-two contrast unresolved).

**What would justify a general-ML main-track paper?** A reusable, pre-specified diagnostic/estimand (e.g. ranking-resolution under dependence) shown on more than one dependence structure with seed-level variability and a control that separates protocol effects from ordinary seed noise (see `NOVELTY_BOUNDARY.md`).
