# Tumor-region mechanism analysis — results
**Status: COMPLETE for the planned subset.** Official tumorMask segmentations (`mask_integrity.csv`, verified) are used **only at analysis time to build modified test inputs**; never in training or standard inference. Final-epoch checkpoints (git-ignored `data/ckpt/`) were used: 44 models = exposure folds 2 & 4 × both models × {CLEAN, CTRL_L100, SIB_L100} × seeds 0,1 (24) + split-seed-101 protocols A/B × 5 folds × both models (20). Raw per-image probabilities: `results/extension/mask/*_variants.csv.gz`; summary: `mask_region_results.csv` (BA & Brier, patient-cluster bootstrap 5,000, seed 2026; exposure analyses use the 2-seed mean-probability ensemble).

Input variants (224², before ImageNet normalisation): full; tumor_only (X·M); tumor_dil_only (tumor + 7 px context); bg_only (tumor + context zeroed); zero_tight (tumor zeroed); zero_shifted (**artifact control**: hole of identical area/shape moved to a non-tumor location).

## Observations
1. **Distribution shift dominates tumor-only inputs.** Networks were trained on full slices; tumor_only BA is .36–.48 (chance .33) in all models — the models cannot classify from the cropped, black-background tumor, so those inputs are mostly out-of-distribution; this does **not** show the tumor is uninformative.
2. **Non-tumor context is highly predictive.** bg_only BA .80–.93 (e.g. split A/B ResNet50: .846/.876; DenseNet121 .803/.861), and zero_shifted ≈ full (.94–.98). Removing the tumor (zero_tight) costs ~.08–.20 BA relative to the equal-area shifted hole (zero_tight − zero_shifted: −.05 to −.20, all CIs exclude 0), so the tumor region carries information beyond a masking artifact, but the rest of the image is also sufficient for most predictions.
3. **Sibling exposure benefit is not confined to tumor input.** SIB − CTRL (BA, 2-seed ensemble): bg_only +.069 (R50 f2), +.049 (R50 f4), +.060 (D121 f2), +.040 (D121 f4) — positive in all four, CIs mostly excluding 0; zero_shifted +.036, −.006, +.050, +.021. tumor_only/tumor_dil_only: mixed in sign (R50 f2 +.067/+.101; D121 f2 +.020/+.032; both fold 4 models −.03 to −.06), consistent with out-of-distribution noise. The full-image SIB−CTRL in this ensemble: +.049, +.001, +.037, +.013.
4. **Protocol B advantage by region (split seed 101).** B−A: full +.037 (R50) / +.035 (D121); bg_only +.029 / +.058; zero_tight +.041 / +.066; zero_shifted +.036 / +.035; tumor_only +.001 (n.s.) / −.064 (A better). The image-level advantage lives mostly in non-tumor content and is absent in tumor-only inputs.

## Evidence-supported conclusion
The data are consistent with models using patient-/acquisition-correlated non-tumor image content (anatomy, skull/background, intensity/field-of-view) that sibling slices share with test slices — but they do **not** show that a model recognises patient identity: non-tumor predictability is expected for any tumor classifier (tumor classes correlate with location/appearance of surrounding anatomy), masking creates large shifts, and no identity-recognition test was run. The result does **not** support a tumor-specific patient mechanism, and any "patient-specific imaging mechanism" claim remains unsupported.
Limitations: final-epoch checkpoints only; 6 hand-designed variants; BA on out-of-distribution inputs is hard to interpret; mask diagnostics were run on a matched subset (not all 172 models); fold-4 sign changes unexplained.
