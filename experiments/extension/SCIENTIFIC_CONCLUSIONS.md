# Scientific conclusions (Phase 3 extension; 172/172 runs, ~22.8 h training compute on one Apple M3 Pro)

1. **Does the image-level advantage persist across new split seeds?** Yes. B−A BA is +.031 to +.047 in all 10 model×seed replicates (3 new split seeds, 2 training seeds, historical), CIs exclude 0 (`SPLIT_SEED_RESULTS.md`, `TRAINING_SEED_RESULTS.md`).
2. **Does the ResNet50/DenseNet121 ranking reversal replicate?** No. 0/3 new split seeds and 1/2 extra training seeds show the historical B-protocol ResNet50 lead; ranking differences are ≤ .010 BA with CIs including 0 (one borderline uncorrected case). The reversal is noise.
3. **Training-seed contribution to ranking variation:** the ranking sign under B flips with training seed alone; BA SD across training seeds (.002–.004) matches split-seed SD.
4. **Does sibling exposure improve classification beyond matched other-patient controls?** For exposed patients, SIB−CTRL ΔBA was positive in 16/16 model×fold×level cells (CI excluding 0 in 7/16); swap (CTRL−CLEAN) ≈ 0 (+.003 mean). But heterogeneous: fold 2 +.045 to +.09, fold 4 ≈ 0–.03. No consistent spillover to unexposed patients.
5. **Dose-response:** not demonstrated. Slope of the sibling contrast over 25→100% is ≈0 in every cell; pooled −.010 (−.073, .031), Holm p = 1.0.
6. **Consistency across models/folds/seeds:** direction consistent, magnitude not (ResNet50 fold 4 ≈ 0). Only 2 of the 5 folds and 2 training seeds were used.
7. **Does tumor-mask analysis support a patient-specific imaging mechanism?** No. Non-tumor regions are highly predictive and sibling benefit appears with tumor removed/non-tumor input (bg_only), but tumor-only inputs are dominated by distribution shift, and no identity test was done. Consistent with shared non-tumor context; does not establish patient identification.
8. **Survives multiple-comparison correction?** In the pre-specified Holm family (4 cells at L=100 + pooled slope), only ResNet50 fold 2 (+.059, p<.001; Holm p<.001). DenseNet121 fold 2 is Holm p = .072; the other two cells and the dose-response slope are not significant. All other analyses are exploratory.
9. **What is new relative to prior MRI patient-leakage work?** (a) A split-seed- and training-seed-replicated, margin-matched patient-disjoint vs image-level comparison on a public brain-tumor MRI set, showing leakage inflation is ~+.03–.05 BA, stable, while the apparent model-ranking reversal does not replicate; (b) a controlled sibling-exposure intervention with matched other-patient controls, a design not found in prior leakage papers I checked (no systematic literature search was redone here). The exposure finding is limited, heterogeneous and partly underpowered; novelty claims should be modest.
10. **Main-track readiness (MICCAI / general ML):** Not yet. Strengths: replicated, pre-registered, honest negative results. Weaknesses: one public dataset with 233 PIDs and no independent external validation; exposure effect confirmed (Holm) in 1 of 4 primary cells, no dose-response, 2 folds/2 seeds only; mechanism unresolved. Appropriate venues: workshop/short paper, or a reproducibility-/benchmark-style submission; a main-track claim would need external validation, all 5 folds for exposure, more seeds and a mechanistic test (e.g. patient-ID probe).

## Negative / null findings
Ranking reversal not reproducible; no dose-response; no spillover; swap null; tumor-only inputs uninformative (OOD); external validation blocked.
## Caveat
SIB arms intentionally contain train/test patient overlap (intervention). Author-confirmed 233 PIDs = 233 individuals (original DICOM unavailable); the 233-ID evaluation is primary.
